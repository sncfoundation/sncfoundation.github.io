Why Sheeternetes

Kubernetes solved container orchestration by adding a distributed operating system, a consensus database, and sixty-three resource types. Sheeternetes solves it by opening a spreadsheet. One of these you can explain to your CFO.

🧾

Zero infrastructure

The control plane is a Google Sheet. Nothing to provision, patch, or page you at 3am. Google keeps the lights on; you keep the tab open.

🚫

No YAML

Workloads are rows. If you must serialize, we emit JSON — which is merely YAML that has already compiled. Indentation-related outages: eliminated.

🗄️

No etcd

State lives in cells. The source of truth is a thing your whole company can already read, filter, and accidentally sort by column B.

🔁

It reconciles

A real control loop: heartbeat-driven scheduling, self-healing on node loss, restart-on-crash, and rolling updates with maxUnavailable=1.

🕸️

Services & DNS (Sheetlium)

A shared Docker network with service aliases gives you service discovery and basic load balancing — Services, without the CRDs.

🚀

GitOps (Sheetlux CD)

Point ./sheetlux at a directory of manifests and it syncs desired state into the Sheet. Declarative delivery, spreadsheet-native.

👀

Observability by staring

Your dashboard is the Deployments tab. Conditional formatting is your alerting stack. Mean time to insight: one glance.

💸

$0 control-plane bill

The most cost-optimized orchestrator in the industry, because the industry forgot to check whether a spreadsheet counts. It does now.

How it works, in one breath

Apps Script is the API server, scheduler, and reconcile loop. The Google Sheet (tabs: Deployments, Nodes, Pods, Events) is etcd. kubelet.sh on your Docker hosts is the kubelet; skctl is your kubectl. Nodes heartbeat the API server, the scheduler bin-packs by CPU and memory, and the loop keeps desired state and reality in agreement. It is exactly Kubernetes, if Kubernetes were a spreadsheet and also a joke.
